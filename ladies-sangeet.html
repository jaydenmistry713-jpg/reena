A Ladies Sangeet is the women’s celebration held in the days before a Punjabi or Pakistani wedding, built around singing, dholki rhythms and dancing. We provide female singers who lead the evening from start to finish — traditional boliyan and folk songs through to Bollywood and modern fusion — for celebrations across the UK and worldwide.

What a Ladies Sangeet evening looks like

A Sangeet is not a background-music event. It is a participation event, and it lives or dies on whether the room joins in. The performers have to know when to hold a song back, when to pull the bride’s side and the groom’s side into the teasing boliyan, and when to let the dholki carry the room into dancing.

That is the part experience buys you. Reena Kaur has been performing at Sangeet nights for over twenty-five years, and the evening is led rather than simply played. Songs are sequenced so the energy builds, families are drawn in by name where you want them to be, and the traditional call-and-response sections actually get a response.

Building the set list with you

Custom song selections are standard, not an upgrade. Most families have a handful of songs that matter — a mother’s song, a grandmother’s song, the one that always gets played — and those go into the running order deliberately rather than by chance.

If there are songs you specifically do not want, tell us those too. It is a short conversation and it is the difference between a set that fits your family and a generic one.

Who books a Ladies Sangeet

Most bookings are the bride’s family, for the Sangeet or dholki night in the run-up to the wedding. We also perform at Mehndi nights, Jago celebrations, engagement parties, milestone birthdays and women’s community events where the same format works.

The scale varies enormously — from a front room with close family to a full function suite — and the line-up is matched to it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Ladies Sangeet?

A Ladies Sangeet is the women’s celebration held in the days before a Punjabi or Pakistani wedding. It centres on singing, dholki playing and dancing, traditionally with the bride’s and groom’s families exchanging teasing folk songs called boliyan. It is one of the most informal and most participatory events in the wedding week.

Are all your Ladies Sangeet performers female?

The singers who lead a Ladies Sangeet are female. Accompanying musicians, including dholki players, are not always. If the evening needs to be women only throughout, tell us when you enquire and we will confirm what we can arrange for your date.

Can we choose the songs?

Yes, and we would encourage it. The set list is built with you in advance from traditional Punjabi folk, boliyan, Pakistani classics, Bollywood and modern fusion. Specific family songs can be written into the running order, and songs you would rather avoid can be left out.

Do you bring sound equipment?

Yes. Professional sound equipment is included and we set it up ourselves.

How far in advance should we book a Ladies Sangeet?

Around twelve months ahead is the safe answer for wedding-season dates, which go early. Shorter notice is still worth asking about — we take last-minute bookings when availability allows.

What is a dholki night, and is it the same as a Sangeet?

In most families they are the same evening under two names. The dholki is the small two-headed hand drum the singing is built on, and many families call the whole night the dholki rather than the Sangeet. If you are booking a dholki night, that is exactly what this service covers.

Do you travel outside the UK for a Sangeet?

Yes. We perform across the UK and internationally, and travel costs are built into the quote up front.
