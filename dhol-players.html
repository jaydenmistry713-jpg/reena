Dhol Players for Hire

Live dhol for baraat entries, receptions and dance sets — the sound that gets a room on its feet.

  1. Home
  2. Services
  3. Dhol Players

The dhol is a double-headed Punjabi drum played with two sticks, and it is the sound most associated with Punjabi celebration. We supply professional dhol players for baraat entries, wedding receptions, Sangeet nights, melas and corporate events across the UK and internationally.

Where the dhol earns its place

The baraat is the obvious one. A groom’s procession without dhol is just a group of people walking, and the drum is what turns an arrival into an entrance.

It works just as well for the couple’s entry into a reception, for cutting through the lull after a seated meal, and for driving a dance set in a way that recorded music does not. The dhol is also a genuinely portable instrument, so it can move with a procession rather than staying tied to a stage.

What a dhol booking includes

  • ✓Professional dhol players, in coordinated performance dress.
  • ✓Performance for the segments you need — baraat, entrances, dance sets — rather than a fixed block dropped into the day.
  • ✓Coordination with your venue, planner and anyone else performing, so entries happen on cue.
  • ✓The option to combine dhol with live singers or a DJ set, booked together.

How many dhol players?

It depends on the space and the effect you want. A single player carries an intimate indoor entrance. A larger group gives a baraat real weight and works better outdoors, where sound disperses.

We bring as many players as the event calls for and the team is chosen for the booking, so this is something we settle when we talk through your day rather than something you have to specify up front.

Combining dhol with the rest of the entertainment

Dhol is frequently booked alongside our folk singers or DJ, and there is a practical reason to book them together: the handovers get planned. A dhol entry that runs straight into a DJ set, or a live singer accompanied by dhol, needs the performers to have agreed the cues in advance. Booking separately tends to mean nobody owns that.

Your line-up

The line-up is chosen for the booking rather than taken off a shelf. We bring as many performers as the event calls for, and the team is picked to suit the style and scale of your celebration.

Frequently asked questions

What is a dhol?

The dhol is a double-headed drum from Punjab, played with two sticks and worn on a strap so the player can move. It produces the loud, driving rhythm associated with bhangra and Punjabi celebration, and because it is portable it can lead a procession.

How many dhol players do we need?

It depends on the space and the moment. A single player suits an intimate indoor entrance; a larger group gives an outdoor baraat much more impact. We bring as many players as the event calls for and agree it with you when discussing the day.

Do you play for baraat processions?

Yes, baraat entries are one of the most common dhol bookings. We coordinate with your venue and planner so the procession starts on cue.

Can we book dhol players alongside singers or a DJ?

Yes, and it is worth doing. Booking together means the handovers between a dhol entry, a live set and a DJ are planned in advance rather than improvised on the day.

Do you travel for dhol bookings?

Yes, across the UK and internationally. Travel costs are built into the quote.

How much do dhol players cost?

Pricing is bespoke and quoted per event. It depends on the number of players, what they are performing for and the travel involved, so we quote against your actual requirements rather than a price list.

Ready to talk about your event?

Tell us the date, the venue and what you have in mind, and we will put together a bespoke quote.
